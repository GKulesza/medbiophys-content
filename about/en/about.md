### What is biophysics?

Biophysics is an interdisciplinary field of science that applies the laws, theories, and methods of physics to explain biological phenomena at all levels of life's organization – from individual atoms and molecules, through cells, tissues, and organs, to whole organisms and ecosystems. Its main goal is the **quantitative understanding of life processes**: not only describing *what* happens in a biological system, but above all answering the questions *how* and *why* these processes occur in accordance with the fundamental laws of physics.

Biophysics assumes that living organisms are subject to the same physical laws as all of inanimate nature. What is crucial here is the transition from descriptive to quantitative understanding – biophysicists create mathematical models that make it possible to calculate and predict the properties of biological systems: forces, energies, diffusion rates, flows, and conformational changes of macromolecules. Unlike biochemistry, which focuses on chemical composition and reactions (answering the questions *of what?* and *with what does it react?*), biophysics studies the **physical mechanisms** underlying them. Biochemistry provides the "vocabulary" of life – molecules and reactions; biophysics explains how physical laws govern those molecules, creating a coherent picture of how organisms function.

### The scope of biophysics – from molecules to consciousness

Modern biophysics covers an extremely broad spectrum of topics that can be organized according to levels of organization of living matter:

**At the molecular level**, biophysics studies intermolecular forces – van der Waals interactions, hydrogen bonds, electrostatic and hydrophobic forces – that shape the spatial structures of proteins, nucleic acids, and lipids. It analyzes the dynamics of these structures, conformational transitions (e.g. protein folding, DNA topology), and the action of individual molecular machines – enzymes, ion pumps, and biological motors that convert chemical energy into mechanical work.

**At the cellular level**, biophysics deals with the properties of biological membranes – their fluidity, electrical conductivity, transport mechanisms across membranes, and bioelectric phenomena such as resting potential and action potential. It studies bioenergetic processes – photosynthesis, cellular respiration, ATP synthesis – describing the flow of electrons and protons in terms of non-equilibrium thermodynamics. It also analyzes diffusion and molecular transport, Brownian motion, and dynamics in the microworld of microorganisms, where viscous forces dominate over inertia.

**At the level of systems and organisms**, biophysics enters the realm of biomechanics – it studies the mechanical properties of tissues (elasticity, viscoelasticity, strength), describes the flow of biological fluids (hemodynamics), analyzes the function of sensory organs (vision, hearing), and the mechanisms of muscle contraction. It applies physical models to describe neural networks, learning, and memory, and even reaches toward the physical foundations of consciousness.

**At the environmental level**, biophysics studies the influence of physical factors – temperature, pressure, radiation, electromagnetic fields – on organisms and evolutionary adaptations to these conditions, with particular attention to radiological protection and the effects of environmental contamination.

### Biophysics in medicine

Biophysics is especially important in the medical sciences. It provides the physical foundations for:

- **Medical imaging** – magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), ultrasonography (USG), nuclear medicine (PET, SPECT).
- **Radiotherapy and radiological protection** – cancer treatment planning, dosimetry, safety principles when working with ionizing radiation.
- **Clinical electrophysiology** – electrocardiography (ECG), electroencephalography (EEG), nerve–muscle conduction studies.
- **Tissue engineering and biomaterials** – design of implants, artificial tissues, and prostheses.
- **Pharmacy and drug design** – understanding how drug molecules interact with target proteins.

### Department of Medical Biophysics, MUB – science, teaching, practice

The Department of Medical Biophysics at the Medical University of Białystok is a unit that for decades has formed a bridge between physics and medicine. We are heirs to a long tradition – our roots reach back to the beginnings of the Medical Academy in Białystok in 1950. Over the years we have evolved with the progress of medical sciences, transforming from a classical Department of Physics into a modern Department of Medical Biophysics that combines experience with openness to the latest achievements of physics in the service of health.

Our mission is not only to pass on knowledge, but above all to shape in future physicians and medical specialists an understanding of the physical foundations of life processes and modern diagnostic and therapeutic techniques. We are present in the education of students in medicine, radiology, and other health sciences.

#### Teaching – from the laws of physics to clinical practice

The laboratory classes and lectures we conduct equip students with practical skills and theoretical knowledge essential in modern medicine. Our teaching program comprises three pillars that reflect the most important areas of medical biophysics:

**1. Optics in medicine**
We teach how light can be a diagnostic and therapeutic tool. Students learn the principles of refractometry, polarimetry, and absorption spectrophotometry (the Bouguer–Lambert–Beer law), study the properties of lenses – which directly translates into understanding the structure and optical defects of the human eye. They explore optical microscopy, resolving power and microscope aperture, and the physical principles of laser operation, which are finding ever broader application in surgery, ophthalmology, and dermatology. This part of teaching is a direct reflection of biophysical research on the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with biological matter.

**2. Electromedicine and the biophysics of the senses**
This part of the course concerns electrical and acoustic phenomena in the human body. Students analyze the physical foundations of electrocardiography (ECG) – recording the heart's electric field, calculate heart rate, and determine the electrical axis of the heart. They learn to use an oscilloscope to observe electrical waveforms, study the mechanisms of voice production and hearing (audiometry, tympanometry), and learn non-invasive methods of measuring blood pressure and blood flow using the Doppler effect. In this way, the theory of bioelectricity and hydrodynamics translates into practical diagnostic skills.

**3. Ionizing radiation and radiological protection**
In the era of nuclear medicine and radiotherapy, this knowledge is crucial for the safety of patients and staff. We teach students the basics of dosimetry – the concepts of exposure dose, absorbed dose, and dose equivalent. We teach the construction and operating principles of radiation detectors (Geiger–Müller counters, scintillation detectors, semiconductor detectors), the physics of radioactive decay, and principles of radiation protection. Students determine radiation attenuation coefficients for various materials, which has practical application in choosing shielding, and learn epidemiological models of the effects of irradiation – deterministic and stochastic. This area of teaching translates directly into work in X-ray diagnostics, radiotherapy, and nuclear medicine.

#### Research activity

The team of the Department of Medical Biophysics conducts advanced experimental research focused on several key areas of contemporary medical biophysics:

- **Biophysics of the circulatory system** – we study the mechanisms of vascular contraction and the influence of trace elements (e.g. cadmium) and vitamins (e.g. L-ascorbic acid) on cardiovascular function. We analyze the molecular basis of disorders such as oxidative stress and endothelial dysfunction, which is directly relevant to understanding the pathogenesis of hypertension and atherosclerosis.

- **Radiobiology and environmental protection** – we assess radiological hazards in the natural environment and their potential impact on population health. We study the presence of radioactive isotopes (e.g. radiocaesium) in natural products such as edible mushrooms, and model the long-term effects of radiation exposure in the context of nuclear accidents. This work fits into the international early warning system for nuclear accidents, which the Department joined as early as 1993.

- **Methodology of teaching medical physics** – we develop modern handbooks, teaching materials, and laboratory protocols that integrate physical phenomena with clinical practice, continually modernizing the educational process and adapting it to the challenges of contemporary medicine.

#### Mission and values

Our overarching goal is to make students aware that physical laws – optics, electricity, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, nuclear physics – underlie most life processes and all modern medical techniques. We train not only skills in operating equipment, but above all **analytical thinking** and the ability to describe phenomena quantitatively – qualities essential for every physician who must make diagnostic and therapeutic decisions based on reliable data.

In our work we are guided by values that define the identity of the Department:

- **Interdisciplinarity** – we combine physics, biology, and medicine, creating space for exchange of ideas among scientists of different specialties.
- **Quality of education** – we continually improve teaching programs to meet the challenges of contemporary medicine.
- **Patient safety** – we teach responsible use of medical technologies, emphasizing radiological protection and professional ethics.
- **Openness to new challenges** – we follow the latest achievements of world biophysics and implement them in our teaching and research work.
